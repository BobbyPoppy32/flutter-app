**The charge:** The case fails as stated: it should not go ahead the way it is described.

## Charges that stuck

1. The app tells a senior "a coordinator will call you", but no coordinator exists. All 8 guilty jurors leaned on this.
2. "Supervised test" is never written down: there is no facilitator, script, consent step, group size or pass mark, and the defense admitted it. All 8 guilty jurors leaned on this.
3. Real seniors would type in their real name, phone number and health needs, and nobody tells them the volunteers and visits are samples. 5 jurors leaned on this.

## What the defense got right

1. Nothing has been tested with a real senior, volunteer or family member. A watched session is the only way to close that gap, and the one human walkthrough so far found four problems on its own.
2. With no backend and only sample people, no real stranger can be sent to anyone's door. The missing vetting, insurance and supply limit what this test can prove, but they put no one at risk.

## Conditions for acquittal

1. Write a one-page test plan. Limit it to moderated sessions on one phone with sample data and no real errands. Name the facilitator, the number of participants and what counts as a pass.
2. Add a consent step that each participant signs. It should say the volunteers, checks, visits and coordinator are not real, and that no real personal or health details should be entered.
3. Reword "a coordinator will call you" and the "we'll tell you" lines, or name the facilitator as the person who stands behind them.

## The sentence

The court finds the app ready but the test not. Put the plan and the consent form on paper, fix the coordinator line, and book a named facilitator. Then sit real people down with the sample visits and watch them.
